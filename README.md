For the Education project, I used two datasets to answer the question: Does the community you live in affect the ACT scores that you may get? The datasets that were used for my project were EdGap and CCD. The EdGap dataset gave the five socioeconomic factors that are used in this study to compare with the other dataset that I used, CCD. This dataset gave information about the schools and students and what their ACT scores were. For this project, these two datasets had to be combined together to make a larger dataset so that it would be easier to do the regression. But before that, we had to clean up the data and get rid of any columns that were not important for the project.
While thinking about the question for this project and needing to use one of the five socioeconomic factors, I wanted to know the comparison between people who grew up in lower-income places and places with higher unemployment, and whether their ACT scores would be affected by these circumstances. Once the data was clean and I could do a regression, I chose to use the examples I saw in class and the results I got from my before-class assignment and put them together. Since in class we did financial income and how that affects ACT scores, we also saw that income was a very big part of the results. When doing the reduced model, it was almost the same as if we had just done the normal, whole model.
For this project, I used multiple regression charts and plots to find that it cannot be claimed that the community, using both income and unemployment, could truly affect the outcome of ACT scores. Median income had some effect, but nothing too drastic. Adding unemployment rates evened out the increase associated with median income, showing that, in the end, it doesn't really matter. The results are inconclusive together but apart it does affect the ACT score with unemployment, or with the median income.
